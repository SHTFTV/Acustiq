ARCHITECTURAL CEILING IDEAS

Wood slats or cassette baffles? Start with the space

Compare real ceiling details from BMO Lougheed, a Robson office and Teck Resources—then separate appearance, access and acoustic requirements.

ACUSTIQ Editorial · October 21, 2026

Use the room as your starting point

Ceilings influence the view from an entrance, the feeling of scale and the relationship between work areas. The supplied BMO Lougheed Mall photographs show a custom wood slat installation. The Robson office images show another wood slat ceiling, while the Teck Resources photographs identify a custom cassette baffle system at Bentall 5. Each set belongs to a different project. Compare the visual character without treating the systems as interchangeable.

Review the pattern from normal viewpoints

Ask to see how the slat direction or baffle pattern will read from entrances, reception positions and occupied areas. Consider changes in level and the way the design meets walls or glazing. A reflected ceiling plan is useful, but viewpoints at eye level help explain the intended result. The construction-stage images also show why perimeter details need attention before the installation is complete.

Keep acoustic claims separate from appearance

A ceiling may have an acoustic purpose, a visual purpose or both. These photographs do not establish absorption data, a room reverberation target or measured results. Ask for the selected product documentation and a project-specific acoustic review where sound control matters. Wood appearance alone does not demonstrate acoustic performance; the construction, backing, coverage and surrounding room all influence the design decision.

Plan lights, services and access together

Coordinate the ceiling pattern with lighting, ventilation, sprinklers and maintenance access. Identify components that may need to be removed later and ask how that will be done. Review support and fixing requirements with the responsible designer and installer. The right visual choice should also allow the building team to operate and maintain the space.

Prepare a ceiling brief

Include room use, dimensions from the current plans, ceiling height, reference images, preferred materials and any product already selected. Describe acoustic goals separately from the look you want. Ask the project team to confirm system suitability, installation requirements and budget. These supplied examples illustrate choices; they do not establish a product endorsement, a universal specification or a quoted installation price.
